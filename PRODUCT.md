# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

AI and ML engineers shipping production LLM applications. They work in fast-moving teams where LLM API costs can spike unexpectedly, latency regressions kill user trust, and there is no existing tooling that makes this observable out of the box. They are skeptical of marketing claims and respond to code, numbers, and specificity.

## Product Purpose

Gantry is invisible infrastructure for AI. Wrap a model client in one line of code and get real-time cost, token, and latency observability for every LLM call in production. A Model Advisor layer automatically routes traffic to cheaper models without sacrificing quality. The product succeeds when an engineer can look at a dashboard and see exactly what their AI features cost, in real time, with no additional instrumentation.

## Brand Personality

Precise · Technical · Invisible

The voice is spare and exact. It earns trust through specificity (real numbers, real code) not enthusiasm. Gantry doesn't shout; it shows you the data and gets out of the way.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI SaaS**: purple gradients, glowing orb hero sections, particle mesh backgrounds, vague "supercharge your AI" copy, animated abstract blobs. Gantry is an engineering tool, not a vibe.
- **Startup Hustle**: confetti, testimonial carousels, emoji in headings, social proof badge stacks, "used by 10,000+ teams" chips with no sourcing. Engineers distrust this register instantly.
- **Enterprise Heavy**: navy and white corporate palette, security and compliance logo grids, white-paper lead capture, sales-speak phrasing like "enterprise-grade" or "mission-critical". Gantry is developer-led, not top-down.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't narrate.** Code examples and real product screenshots carry the argument. Avoid abstract claims about making AI "better" or "easier"; show the one-liner, show the dashboard number.
2. **Instrument-grade precision.** Every data point, label, and UI element should feel exact — monospace coordinates, sharp radii, tight spacing. The aesthetic signals that Gantry treats numbers seriously.
3. **Invisible by design.** The product disappears into the stack; the landing page should feel the same. No decorative noise that competes with the product's own data. Dark backgrounds keep focus on signal.
4. **Developer vocabulary.** Write for engineers who skip marketing copy. Technical specificity wins over adjective stacking. Concrete verbs over superlatives.
5. **Friction is a bug.** Layout and information architecture should feel inevitable. The path from "what is this?" to "I want to try it" should require no cognitive work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Hard requirements: ≥4.5:1 contrast for body text, ≥3:1 for large/bold text, keyboard navigable, ARIA labels on interactive elements. Reduced-motion alternative for every animation (typically opacity crossfade). No color-only information encoding.
